Miles Tracker — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 27 August 2026

Miles Tracker is a personal credit-card spending, expense and portfolio tracker. It runs entirely in your web browser. There is no application server operated by us: the app talks only to Google's own services, using your own Google account, and nobody else — including the developer — can read your data.

This policy explains exactly what Google data the app accesses, why, where it goes, and how to remove it.

Miles Tracker is an independent, non-commercial project by an individual developer. It is not affiliated with Google, nor with any bank or card issuer whose emails it reads. Contact: aiksiang.ang@gmail.com.

Google account data the app accesses

Scope requestedWhat it is used for

How Gmail access is used, specifically

The app does not read your mailbox generally. It issues a narrow Gmail search restricted to messages that are both:

  • from a fixed list of bank and payment sender domains (DBS, POSB, UOB, OCBC, HSBC, Citibank, Maybank, Standard Chartered, American Express, Instarem, Trust Bank, GXS, MariBank and Grab), and
  • carrying one of eight transaction-alert subject terms — "transaction", "spent", "alert", "charged", "debited", "ibanking", "internet banking", or "Your GrabPay Wallet Statement" — and
  • dated within the month you are currently viewing.

All three conditions must hold. In full, the search the app sends to Gmail is:

from:("dbs.com" OR "posb.com" OR "uob.com" OR "uobgroup.com" OR "ocbc.com"
      OR "hsbc.com" OR "citi.com" OR "citibank.com" OR "maybank.com" OR "sc.com"
      OR "aexp.com" OR "instarem.com" OR "trustbank.sg" OR "gxs.com"
      OR "maribank.sg" OR "no-reply@grab.com")
subject:("transaction" OR "spent" OR "alert" OR "charged" OR "debited"
      OR "ibanking" OR "internet banking" OR "Your GrabPay Wallet Statement")
after:YYYY/MM/DD before:YYYY/MM/DD

Mail that does not match every part of this is never requested and therefore never reaches the app at all. The date bounds are the month you are viewing, widened by one day at the start to account for the time-zone difference between Gmail's filters and Singapore time.

Matching messages are fetched into your browser's memory and parsed there to extract four things: the amount, the merchant name, the last four digits of the card, and the date. The message body is then discarded. Email content is never transmitted to any server, never stored, and never seen by the developer or any third party. The app never sends, modifies, deletes or labels mail; the scope is read-only and cannot do so.

How Sheets/Drive access is used, specifically

The app reads the statement, investment-history, investment-value, price and class tabs of the one spreadsheet you link, and writes back: reconciliation match results, an Expenses YYYY-MM export tab, and the investment class labels you choose. The spreadsheet stays yours and remains fully editable by you at any time.

The per-file nature of this scope is enforced by Google, not merely a restraint the app observes. Access is recorded against specific file identifiers, so a request for any other file is refused by Google's servers regardless of what the app asks for. The broad "see and manage all your Drive files" scope is a different permission, and this app does not request it.

Every connection the app makes

Because the app has no server of its own, the complete set of network destinations it contacts is short enough to list. These are the only Google endpoints it calls:

EndpointPurpose
sheets.googleapis.com/v4/spreadsheets Create, read and write the one spreadsheet you linked
www.googleapis.com/gmail/v1/users/me/messages The filtered transaction-alert search described above, and reading the messages it returns
identitytoolkit.googleapis.com Firebase sign-in with your Google account
securetoken.googleapis.com Refreshing the sign-in token so you are not signed out constantly
firestore.googleapis.com Saving and loading your own settings and decisions, as described below

Notably absent is the Google Drive API. The app calls it nowhere — it never lists, searches, browses or enumerates files, and contains no code capable of doing so. It addresses your spreadsheet directly by its identifier through the Sheets API.

The only request outside that list is to fonts.googleapis.com and fonts.gstatic.com for the page's typography, which carries none of your data. There are no other outbound connections of any kind.

Where your data is stored

In your browser

Transactions parsed from email, loaded statement rows, the investment ledger and price quotes are cached in your browser's localStorage so the app does not re-fetch on every visit. Access tokens are held in sessionStorage and expire when the browser tab closes. All of this is on your own device. Signing in with a different Google account on the same device automatically discards the previous account's cached data.

In Google Cloud Firestore

Your decisions and settings — not your email — are saved to a Google Cloud Firestore database so they follow you between devices. This is limited to:

  • Card settings and expense categories you define
  • Merchant-to-category mappings and per-transaction overrides you make
  • Transactions you hide, and transactions you enter manually or as recurring
  • The ID of the spreadsheet you linked, its card-label mapping, and your reconciliation decisions
  • Your Google account identifier and email address, from sign-in

Each record is stored under your own account identifier, and the database's security rules permit read and write access only to the signed-in owner of that record. The database is hosted by Google Cloud on Google's infrastructure.

To be straightforward about one thing: as the owner of the underlying Google Cloud project, the developer holds administrative access to that database and could in principle view the settings records listed above. That access exists to operate the service, not to inspect anyone's data. It does not extend to your email — which is never stored there — nor to your spreadsheet, which lives in your own Drive under your own account.

What the app does not do

  • It does not sell, rent, trade or share your data with anyone.
  • It does not use your data for advertising, profiling or credit assessment.
  • It does not use your data to train, fine-tune or evaluate any artificial intelligence or machine-learning model.
  • It contains no analytics, tracking pixels, advertising SDKs or third-party trackers of any kind. The only outbound requests are to Google's APIs and to Google Fonts for typography.
  • Nobody reads your email. Message content never leaves your browser, so it is technically unreachable by the developer or anyone else.

Limited Use disclosure

Miles Tracker's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

In particular, Google user data is used only to provide and improve the user-facing features described above; it is not transferred to others except as necessary to provide those features, to comply with applicable law, or as part of a merger or acquisition; and it is not used for serving advertisements or for developing or improving generalised AI or ML models.

Data retention and deletion

You can remove your data at any time, and you do not need to ask us to do it:

  • Revoke access. Visit myaccount.google.com/permissions, select Miles Tracker, and choose Remove Access. The app immediately loses all ability to read your mail or your spreadsheet.
  • Clear the local cache. Clear site data for the app in your browser, or use the reset controls in the app's Settings. This removes every parsed transaction and cached figure from your device.
  • Delete stored settings, from inside the app. Open Settings → Data and choose Delete all my data. This permanently erases every settings and transaction record held for your account on the server, along with the local caches on your device. It takes effect immediately, needs no request to anyone, and cannot be undone.
  • Your spreadsheet belongs to you and is unaffected by any of the above; delete it from Google Drive if you no longer want it.

Cached email-derived data is transient by design: it is recomputed from Gmail on demand and can be discarded at any point with no loss of your settings.

Security

All communication with Google APIs uses HTTPS. Authorisation uses Google OAuth 2.0; the app never sees or stores your Google password. Access tokens are short-lived and held only for the duration of a browser session. Stored settings are protected by per-user Firestore security rules that deny access to everyone except the record's owner.
